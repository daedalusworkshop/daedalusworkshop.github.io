The chain

02

Four stages, and the model-free version of each so you can watch it think. The taste lives in the table, not the model.

Stage 1 · the scorer

Sentence in, coordinates out.

  • valenceunpleasant → pleasant
  • arousalstill → activated
  • dominancesmall → in control

A small built-in word list carries valence, arousal and dominance for each word it knows. Your sentence is the average of the words that scored. Words it does not know fall back to neutral and are shown greyed out, so you can see exactly what it heard.

Stage 2 · the ring

Coordinates become a place.

Russell’s circumplex arranges emotions in a circle: pleasant right, unpleasant left, activated up, still down. Your point lands somewhere on it and the nearest named region claims it.

feeds

Stage 3 · the performer

The emotion picks its player.

A fixed Juslin-style table, designed once by a human: anger plays fast, loud, sharp and staccato; sadness plays slow, quiet, low and legato; tenderness plays slow, soft and warm; fear plays fast, quiet, high and trembling. Dominance nudges loudness; arousal nudges note count.

feeds

Stage 4 · the synth

Parameters become sound.

Plain Web Audio oscillators. Tempo sets the beat, mode picks the scale, register places the home pitch, loudness sets gain, articulation sets note length, roughness detunes a second oscillator and sharpens the dissonance, and the contour follows valence: rising for bright sentences, falling for heavy ones. Tense emotions refuse to resolve.

Lines modify,
not restart

03

“i want a cat” states a chord. “who is black” modifies it. So the piece keeps a rolling state: the first line you play sets the home pitch and the pulse, and every later line plays against them. The green rows in the performer table are what was held; everything else is what this line changed.

Try it: play i want a cat, then who is black. Same pulse, same home, darker room.

What’s simplified

04
The lexicon is tiny.A few hundred hand-scored words versus the 20,000 in the full NRC VAD list. Averaging ignores grammar, so “not bad, actually good” blurs together. Unknown words are neutral and visible, never silently skipped.
The ring is a circle.Regions are angular slices of Russell’s space. Real emotion categories are messier, overlapping and personal.
The performer table is seven knobs.Juslin’s performer studies track far more: timing micro-deviation, vibrato, phrasing, onset noise. Here it is tempo, mode, register, loudness, timbre, articulation, roughness.
The synth is oscillators.No samples, no body, no room. Enough to feel the mapping move, not enough to be the instrument.
Perceived, not felt.The field guide’s first rule: this makes a sentence legible as emotion. It does not promise to make you feel it.
