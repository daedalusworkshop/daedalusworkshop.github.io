The little sun
over i: a lineage

Your dot makes an old human move: turning a point into a source. A dot locates; rays make it act on the world.

01

Aten

Life arriving as light. Aten's rays end in hands, giving breath and life.

The “is” sun inherits light as an active gift, then miniaturizes a deity into punctuation. Keep the rays abstract, not hand-shaped.

The Met
02

Japan

Nation, dawn, and a live political echo: Japan as the “source of the sun.”

A plain disk feels elemental. Red and white, sixteen rays, or rays filling the field quickly echo the imperial Rising Sun flag.

Britannica
03

Zia

Sacred order: four groups of four for directions, seasons, stages of life, and daily obligations.

The “is” mark can share the idea of ordered radiance, but should avoid the exact grouped-four construction without engaging Zia authorship and consent.

Zia Pueblo
04

Halo

Light made visible around a person: awakened or holy presence, not the literal sun.

Your i-dot moves radiance from a body to language itself: “is” illuminated from within.

The Met
05

Sol to gold

Authority, glory, perfection. Sol's crown became royal power; heraldry gave the sun a face; alchemy made ☉ both Sun and gold.

Without face, crown, or gold, “is” reads less as sovereignty and more as potential.

Getty alchemy booklet
06

Modern marks

Optimism engineered. Corporate sparks turn radiance into energy, discovery, and friendliness.

Too symmetric and polished, and history collapses into “innovation.” An irregularity, open gap, or bond to the i-stem keeps it yours.

Walmart Spark
07

Cipher

Identity compressed. Monograms overlap initials until letters become emblem.

“Is” inherits the cipher's recognition game, but the sun adds narrative: not only identity compressed, but the future is bright.
